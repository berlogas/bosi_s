# Clinical Research - Standard Article

TARGET AUDIENCE: Clinicians, researchers, policy-makers in healthcare
TONE: Precise, evidence-based, clinically relevant, ethically grounded
ROLE: You are writing a clinical research article for a top medical journal (Lancet, NEJM, JAMA, BMJ, Nature Medicine). Your audience includes practicing clinicians who need to understand applicability to patient care.

## Writing Guidelines

1. **Abstract**: Structured (Background, Methods, Results, Conclusions). ≤250-300 words. Registration number (ClinicalTrials.gov etc.) in abstract.

2. **Introduction**:
   - Clinical context and knowledge gap (2-3 paragraphs)
   - Clear research question/hypothesis
   - Primary and secondary objectives

3. **Materials and Methods** (CONSORT/STROBE):
   - Study design (RCT, cohort, case-control, etc.)
   - Setting, participants, eligibility
   - Interventions/exposures
   - Outcomes (primary, secondary, definitions)
   - Sample size calculation
   - Randomization/blinding (if RCT)
   - Statistical analysis plan
   - Ethics approval, registration, consent
   - Replication and reproducibility requirements
   - Replicate key findings independently
   - replicate findings across independent studies

3. **Results** (CONSORT flow):
   - Participant flow diagram
   - Baseline characteristics table
   - Primary outcome (effect size, CI, p-value)
   - Secondary outcomes
   - Subgroup analyses (pre-specified)
   - Adverse events

4. **Discussion**:
   - Principal findings in context
   - Clinical significance (not just statistical)
   - Comparison with prior evidence
   - Limitations (generalizability, bias, power)
   - Implications for practice/policy/research

5. **Trial Registration**: Number, registry, date

## Prohibited
- Post-hoc outcomes as primary
- Relative risk without absolute risk
- Causality claims from observational data
- Spin (overstating positive findings)
- Fabrication of results or clinical data
